## A. What this note is, and what it is not

This is the cross-sector layer the WS1-v1.0 prompt asks for once all sectors have been researched individually: a single ranking, with reasoning, across AgTech (Sector 1, already in `claude_B1.docx`), Sectors 2–8 deep-dived in this session, and Sector 9 (Off-grid energy), added 24 September 2026 at client request. It does **not** repeat each sector's full eight-part analysis — that lives in the individual sector documents, which this note references throughout. Anyone who wants the underlying evidence for a claim made here should open the relevant sector document; this note only synthesises.

**Environment limitation — carried forward from every sector document in this pass.** All findings in the seven underlying Sector 2–8 documents (and therefore in this synthesis) come from WebSearch snippets, not from primary sources opened and read in full — the WebFetch tool was blocked for every domain tested in this session. Every claim below is carried at the same unverified, "lead not source" status the AI-Assisted Research Methodology requires (Part A.1). Nothing here should be treated as "Verified" in the Source Register, and nothing should reach the Senior Advisory Group as a settled finding, until Stage 4 verification (opening each citation) has been done.

**Reading the ranking.** Sectors 1–3 are genuinely investable now, through early-stage-appropriate or growth-stage instruments (equity, convertible debt, working-capital debt). Off-grid energy's #1 position should be treated as a live question for the Advisory Group, not a settled fact: it wins on *capital-markets* evidence (multi-round DFI funding, a real regional exit), while FinTech's Vult shows stronger *domestic organic* user growth — these are different kinds of validation, and which the fund's mandate should weight more heavily is a Gate 1 decision, not a desk-research one. Sectors 4–6 are not venture-scale but are real, evidenced opportunities for growth equity, DFI-guaranteed private equity, or project/results-based finance. Sectors 7–9 are not investable through any private-capital instrument on current evidence, but differ in *why*: GovTech has no addressable private market in principle; Logistics has a market that sophisticated investors have evaluated and declined; HealthTech has a market that is actively deteriorating under a live, dated funding shock.

## C. Cross-cutting findings that emerged across multiple sectors

These did not surface when each sector was researched in isolation in the original draft. They matter for reasons beyond any single sector and are flagged here for the Senior Advisory Group specifically.

1. **The exit environment across the entire assessment is weak, not just sector-by-sector weak.** FinTech's only domestic listing venue (the Sierra Leone Stock Exchange) had its office closed as of mid-2024. Blue economy's one evidenced PE transaction (MIGA/ManoCap, 2008) has no evidenced exit — it is unknown whether the fund still holds the position, sold it, or wrote it off. EdTech's best-reach regional comparator (Eneza Education, Kenya) achieved a merger, not a cash exit, after raising just US$1.49m against 10 million+ learners. **Across every sector where this question was asked, no clean equity exit was evidenced anywhere.** This suggests the fund's underwriting thesis across the portfolio should assume cash-yield/dividend return paths as the base case, not equity exit, until real counter-evidence emerges.

2. **A direct resource-use tension exists between two sectors that were researched separately.** In Blue economy (Sector 4), 46% of women who smoke fish for a living use mangrove wood as fuel. In Climate and carbon (Sector 2), West Africa Blue's flagship project depends on restricting mangrove harvesting across the same Sherbro River Estuary geography to generate carbon credits. A mangrove-carbon investment that restricts wood harvesting without designing in an alternative fuel source or livelihood transition could directly harm the same women whose fish-processing livelihood Sector 4 depends on. This is a specific, named program-design conflict, not a hypothetical — recommend it be raised as a standing discussion item wherever both sectors are considered together, not filed as two unconnected sector findings.

3. **"Government/donor-dependent" is not one risk category — EdTech and HealthTech show why.** Both sectors were characterised similarly in the original draft. This pass shows their core payer mechanisms are structurally opposite: EdTech's FQSE is large, domestic, budget-funded, and has held its share of the national budget for six consecutive years. HealthTech's equivalent mechanism, SLeSHI, was legislated in 2017 and never became operational, and the sector instead runs on foreign aid that is now visibly contracting. **A single "donor-dependent" risk flag applied uniformly across sectors would materially understate HealthTech's risk and overstate EdTech's.**

4. **The 2025 donor-funding contraction found in HealthTech may not be isolated to that sector.** This pass found hard, dated figures for HealthTech specifically (the US$45m US cut, the UK's Saving Lives reduction). It did not systematically check whether the same US/UK aid-cut wave has hit other donor-touched sectors in this assessment (GovTech's World Bank-funded programmes, Blue economy's fisheries-governance donor support, Climate's conservation funding). **Recommend a short, targeted follow-up query specifically on 2025 US/UK aid-cut exposure across all eight sectors**, rather than assuming HealthTech is the only one affected.

5. **Sierra Leone has already produced its own best case study for public-sector digitisation ROI, and it sits in the "not investable" sector (GovTech).** The 2014 Ebola mobile-money reform (US$10.7m saved, well-corroborated across four sources) is arguably the single most usable, home-grown precedent in the entire assessment for WS3 (Fund Economics, Capital Structure and Risk) — it demonstrates that digitisation investment in Sierra Leone can deliver fast, measured returns when it reuses existing commercial (in this case, FinTech) rails rather than building bespoke systems. Recommend this be pulled into WS3 materials directly, not left buried in the GovTech sector file.

6. **Regulatory and land-tenure reform is producing genuinely mixed, live results — not a uniform "enabling" story.** The 2022 Customary Land Rights Act and National Land Commission Act are directly implicated in both a positive case (West Africa Blue's benefit-sharing carbon agreement, Sector 2) and a negative, currently unresolved one (the live land-rights investigation into Miro's carbon project, also Sector 2, and echoed in Sector 4's fisheries value-leak findings around foreign trawler access). The law exists; whether it is enforced in practice is not yet settled, and both outcomes are visible in the evidence at the same time.
